# International student support — visa, work rights, settling in

Studying in a new country is a big shift. The International team is here to help with the practical stuff (visas, work, healthcare, accommodation) and the human stuff (homesickness, finding community, knowing where to turn).

## When to use this

Use this article if you're an international student and you need help with:

- Your student visa — extending, varying, or understanding the conditions
- Working while you study — what you're allowed to do, and how many hours
- Health insurance and access to GPs or hospitals
- Finding flatmates, accommodation, or sorting a tenancy issue
- Feeling far from home, or struggling to settle in
- Travel home for an emergency or family event

## Your options

### Talk to the International Student team
First port of call for anything visa-, enrolment-, or settlement-related. Book a 1:1 appointment through the Student Portal (My Support → International). Appointments are free and can be in person, online, or by phone.

### Visa and immigration questions
The International team can help you understand your visa conditions and what to do if your situation changes (changing courses, dropping a paper, working more than allowed). For formal visa advice, you'll be referred to a licensed immigration adviser — the team will tell you when that's needed.

For official visa info, the New Zealand government's immigration website (**immigration.govt.nz**) is the authoritative source.

### Health and wellbeing
If you're unwell, you can call **Healthline** on **0800 611 116** for free health advice in many languages. If you need to talk to someone about mental health, **1737** is free and answered 24/7. Both work from any phone.

### Find your community
The university has student clubs, cultural associations, and buddy programmes for new international students. Browse them through the Student Portal under **Clubs & Community**. The Whānau and Pacific Centres also welcome international students.

### Message Otto
For quick questions — "Where do I get a tax number?", "How do I open a bank account?", "Is my course still on this week?" — message **Otto** any time.

## What happens next

When you book an International appointment, the advisor will read your file beforehand and come prepared. Bring your passport, visa, and any letters or emails relevant to the issue. Most things can be sorted in a single appointment; if it's more complex, the advisor will set up a follow-up.
