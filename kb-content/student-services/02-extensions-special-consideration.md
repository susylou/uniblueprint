# Apply for an assignment extension or special consideration

Life gets in the way sometimes — illness, a family emergency, something that throws your study off course. If you can't get an assignment in on time or you sat an exam while unwell, you can apply for an extension or special consideration.

## When to use this

Apply if:

- You're unwell or injured and it's affecting your study
- Something significant has happened at home (bereavement, family emergency, caring responsibilities)
- A disability or ongoing health condition has flared up
- You've experienced harm or distress that's stopped you working

You don't need to share every detail — just enough for someone to understand the situation.

## Your options

### Short extension (up to 7 days)
For most assignments, you can request a short extension directly through the Student Portal. Go to **My Studies → Request Extension**, pick the assignment, give a brief reason, and submit. Your course coordinator usually responds within 2 working days.

### Longer extension or special consideration
If you need more than a week, or it's an exam or major assessment, lodge a **Special Consideration** request. This goes to the faculty office and may need supporting documentation — a medical certificate, a letter from a counsellor, or similar. Don't stress about getting the paperwork perfect; submit what you have and the team will work with you.

### Not sure which to use?
Message **Otto** through the portal and describe what's going on. Otto can point you to the right form and flag if your situation might need wider support (like a hardship grant or counselling).

## What happens next

You'll get an email confirming your request. For short extensions, a decision usually comes back within 2 working days. Special consideration takes a bit longer — up to 10 working days — because it's reviewed by a small panel. If your request is urgent or you're waiting on a deadline, reply to the confirmation email and someone will chase it.

If your request isn't approved, you can ask for a review. The decision letter explains how.
