# Whānau and pastoral care — Māori and Pacific student support

Aotearoa University's Whānau Centre and Pacific Centre are spaces where Māori and Pacific students belong by default. Drop in for a kōrero, find a quiet study spot, share kai, or get help navigating university life with people who get where you're coming from.

## When to use this

Come in (or get in touch) if you want:

- A space on campus that feels like home
- Support with your studies — from people who understand your context
- Help connecting with scholarships, hardship support, or career pathways
- A first-year mentor or buddy in your second year and beyond
- Time with kaiāwhina (support people) when things are tough
- To get involved in cultural events, kapa haka, Pacific groups, or community activities

You don't need to whakapapa to a specific iwi or come from a specific island to be welcome. If you identify as Māori or Pacific, the door is open.

## Your options

### Visit the Whānau Centre
A dedicated space for Māori students, with kaiāwhina on hand, quiet study areas, kai, and regular events. Just walk in — no appointment needed. Hours and location are on the Student Portal under **Whānau & Community**.

### Visit the Pacific Centre
A dedicated space for Pacific students from across Te Moana-nui-a-Kiwa. Same idea — drop in, study, talk to a Pacific liaison, find your people. Hours and location on the portal.

### One-on-one with a kaiāwhina or Pacific liaison
If you'd rather talk privately about something specific — study stress, family pressure, financial worries, identity stuff — book a confidential meeting through the portal or just ask at the centre.

### Connect through Otto
Not sure where to start? Message **Otto** and ask about Māori or Pacific student support. Otto can introduce you to the right person and book a first meeting.

## What happens next

If you book a one-on-one, you'll meet your kaiāwhina or liaison somewhere comfortable — at the centre, over coffee, or online if that suits better. There's no script. You talk about what's on your mind, and together you work out what (if anything) needs to happen next. Whānau can be invited in if you'd like that.

Everything shared stays confidential. The centres work closely with counsellors, hardship support, and academic advisors — so if you need wider help, it can be arranged without you having to retell your story.
