# Disability and learning support — register for accommodations

If you have a disability, learning difference, mental health condition, or chronic illness that affects your study, we can put practical supports in place — extra time in exams, note-takers, accessible materials, flexible deadlines, and more.

## When to use this

Register if any of these apply:

- A physical disability or chronic health condition
- A learning difference (dyslexia, ADHD, autism, dyspraxia, others)
- A mental health condition that affects study
- Temporary injury or illness lasting more than a few weeks
- Sensory disability (vision, hearing)

You don't need a formal diagnosis to start the conversation. The Accessibility team can help you work out what's needed.

## Your options

### Register with the Accessibility team
This is the main door. Fill in the **Accessibility Registration** form in the Student Portal (My Support → Accessibility). You'll be asked about your situation and what's getting in the way of study. An advisor will get in touch within 5 working days to set up a meeting.

### Bring what you have — or none of it
Supporting documentation helps (medical letter, diagnostic report, GP note), but if you don't have it yet, register anyway. The team can talk you through what's useful and may be able to help you get assessed.

### Already registered? Update your plan
If you've registered before and your needs have changed — new course, condition worsened, new medication — book a review through the portal. Plans should grow with you.

### Quick questions
Message **Otto** for anything quick — "Do I qualify?", "How do I get exam time?", "Who do I talk to about lecture recordings?". Otto can connect you to an advisor if you need a human.

## What happens next

After you register, you'll meet with an Accessibility advisor (in person, online, or by phone — your choice). Together you'll build a **Learning Support Plan** that lists the accommodations you're entitled to. The plan is shared with your course coordinators on a need-to-know basis — your specific diagnosis is not.

Plans are reviewed each year, and you can ask for a change at any time. Everything you share with the Accessibility team is confidential.
