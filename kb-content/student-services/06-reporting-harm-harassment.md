# Reporting harm, harassment or unsafe behaviour

If something has happened to you or someone you know — harassment, bullying, sexual harm, discrimination, threats, or behaviour that doesn't feel right — you can report it. You can also just talk to someone, with no obligation to make a formal complaint.

## When to use this

Use this article if you've experienced or witnessed:

- Sexual harm, harassment, or unwanted contact
- Bullying or intimidation (in person or online)
- Discrimination — on race, gender, sexuality, religion, disability, anything
- Threats, stalking, or feeling unsafe on or near campus
- Concerning behaviour from another student or a staff member

You don't have to be sure it "counts" before you reach out. If something felt wrong, that's enough.

## Your options

### Talk first, decide later
You can have a confidential conversation with a Safer Communities advisor without starting a formal process. They'll listen, explain what your options are, and let you decide what (if anything) happens next. Book through the Student Portal (**My Support → Safety & Wellbeing**) or message **Otto** and ask to talk to someone.

### Make a report — formal or informal
If you want to report something, you can:

- **Informal report**: A confidential record is made and you can ask for the behaviour to be addressed without a formal investigation
- **Formal complaint**: Triggers a university investigation. You'll be supported by an advisor through the process and your wellbeing is checked in on throughout

You can change your mind at any point. The team's job is to support you, not push you.

### If you're in immediate danger
Call **111**. If you're on campus, campus security can also respond quickly — the number is on the back of your student ID and in the portal.

### Specialist national support
For sexual harm, **Safe to talk** is a free 24/7 national helpline run by trained specialists — call, text or webchat. For mental health support, **1737** is free and answered 24/7. Both work from any phone with no credit needed.

## What happens next

If you make a report, you'll be assigned a Safer Communities advisor. They'll explain each step, check in on how you're doing, and won't take action you haven't agreed to (unless someone is at serious risk). You can bring a support person — a friend, whānau member, or counsellor — to any meeting.

What you share is kept confidential. The only exceptions are when someone is at risk of serious harm, or when the law requires us to act.
